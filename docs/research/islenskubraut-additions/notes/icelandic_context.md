## 1. What the national curriculum asks for, how it maps to CEFR, and how schools structure "íslenskubraut" programmes

### Takeaway

The upper-secondary curriculum has no current, published CEFR-aligned framework for Icelandic as a second language. The 2011 Aðalnámskrá framhaldsskóla works in four general skill levels (hæfniþrep), and schools write their own ÍSAN courses. The only detailed CEFR-mapped ÍSAT framework (Forstig, then 1.–3. stig = A1, A2, B1) is the 2022 MMS framework for **grunnskóli**. It is age-independent and is the obvious thing to reuse. Since autumn 2024 three schools, Kvennaskólinn, FMOS and Menntaskólinn á Ísafirði, have run ministry-backed two-year **íslenskubrautir** aimed at A1→B1. Many other schools offer standalone ÍSAN courses.

### Cited Findings

**National curriculum (framhaldsskóli)**

- The 2011 Aðalnámskrá framhaldsskóla places all upper-secondary study on four skill levels (hæfniþrep), which overlap with the compulsory and university levels. Programmes are ranked on these levels. — [Aðalnámskrá framhaldsskóla 2011](https://www.stjornarradid.is/media/menntamalaraduneyti-media/media/ritogskyrslur/adalnskr_frsk_alm_2011.pdf); [namskra.is: hæfniþrep](https://namskra.is/books/adalnamskraframhaldsskola2011/haefnithrep)
- The general rule for stúdentspróf is at least 20 credits in Icelandic, at least 10 of them at the third skill level. According to the search summary, the 2011 text has a special provision for students with Icelandic as a second language, who take at least 5 credits at level 2 in Icelandic. The summary's wording about substituting other subjects is unclear and needs checking in the source. — [Aðalnámskrá framhaldsskóla 2011, 2nd ed. with 2015 amendments](https://www.stjornarradid.is/library/01--Frettatengt---myndir-og-skrar/MRN/Adalnamskra%20framhaldsskola_2_utg_breyt_2015_.pdf)
- The 1999 Icelandic curriculum was the first to give Icelandic as a second language its own final goals and course codes (ÍSA 103, ÍSA 203 …), and a separate "proposals" document for Icelandic as a second language in framhaldsskóli exists from that era. These have been superseded but are the last national, subject-level ISL curriculum text found for framhaldsskóli. — [Aðalnámskrá framhaldsskóla – Íslenska 1999](https://www.stjornarradid.is/media/menntamalaraduneyti-media/media/ritogskyrslur/AFislenska.pdf); [Íslenska sem annað tungumál – Tillögur (námsskipan)](https://www.stjornarradid.is/media/menntamalaraduneyti-media/media/ritogskyrslur/namsskipan_isl_annad_frhsk.pdf)
- Legal basis: under lög 92/2008 (35. gr.), every upper-secondary school must have a reception plan (móttökuáætlun) for students of foreign origin. Students with diverse language and cultural backgrounds, and Icelanders who have lived long abroad, are entitled to teaching in Icelandic as a second language. — [MEMM móttökuáætlun](https://memm.mms.is/mottaka/mottokuaaetlun-2); [Stjórnarráðið – framhaldsskólar](https://www.stjornarradid.is/verkefni/menntamal/framhaldsskolar/); [Lög um framhaldsskóla 92/2008](https://www.althingi.is/lagas/nuna/2008092.html)
- A 2021 parliamentary answer describes the ISL courses in framhaldsskóli as mostly introductory courses in writing, grammar and speaking. It notes that the teachers must hold a master's-level teaching licence, but no ISL-specific qualification is required. — [Þingskjal 1569, 151. löggjafarþing](https://www.althingi.is/altext/pdf/151/s/1569.pdf)

**The CEFR-mapped framework that does exist (grunnskóli, 2022)**

- MMS's 2022 "Hæfnisvið og hæfnirammar í íslensku sem öðru tungumáli" is a companion to the renewed ch. 19 of Aðalnámskrá grunnskóla. It sets out **Forstig, 1. stig, 2. stig, 3. stig**, and the levels follow the student's stage in Icelandic, not age ("Hæfnirammarnir miða ekki við aldur nemandans heldur stöðu hans í íslensku"). **1. stig = A1, 2. stig = A2, 3. stig = B1.** Students are expected to follow the ISL framework for two to four years before moving to the age-based Icelandic curriculum. — [MMS hæfnirammar ÍSAT 2022 (memm.mms.is copy)](https://memm.mms.is/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/haefnirammar_isad_2022.pdf); [mms.is copy](https://mms.is/sites/mms.is/files/haefnisvid_og_haefnirammar_i_isat.pdf)
- That framework defines 1. stig as beginners who are literate in their mother tongue and know the Latin script. 3. stig students "have a good foundation and can concentrate on building vocabulary and developing academic language use (námstengd málnotkun)". It also stresses basic vocabulary (grunnorðaforði) and cites the CEFR Companion Volume. — [MMS hæfnirammar ÍSAT](https://mms.is/sites/mms.is/files/haefnisvid_og_haefnirammar_i_isat.pdf)
- The 2021 revision of Aðalnámskrá grunnskóla added sub-chapters on ISL and on reception, plus a reference timetable of recommended weekly hours by age and proficiency level. The OECD singles this out. — [OECD 2024, Skills and Labour Market Integration of Immigrants and their Children in Iceland](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/09/skills-and-labour-market-integration-of-immigrants-and-their-children-in-iceland_cc5aa7de/96adc300-en.pdf)
- A national CEFR-based ISL assessment (stöðumat) was reported as moving into test development from early 2025. — [Mannamál: Þróun stöðumats í íslensku sem öðru máli](https://mannamal.is/throun-stodumats-i-islensku-sem-odru-mali/)

**Íslenskubrautir: the 2024 programmes**

- In July 2024 the ministry announced new two-year íslenskubrautir starting that autumn at **Kvennaskólinn í Reykjavík, Framhaldsskólinn í Mosfellsbæ (FMOS) and Menntaskólinn á Ísafirði**. Their aims are Icelandic language and cultural skills, social wellbeing and participation, and preparing students for further upper-secondary study and work. The programmes are tied to the **MEMM** development project (Menntun, móttaka, menning), which builds advice, training and access to materials for framhaldsskólar. — [Stjórnarráðið, 1 July 2024: Nýjar íslenskubrautir fyrir nemendur af erlendum uppruna](https://www.stjornarradid.is/efst-a-baugi/frettir/stok-frett/2024/07/01/Nyjar-islenskubrautir-fyrir-nemendur-af-erlendum-uppruna/)
- **Kvennaskólinn:** the brautin is for students of foreign origin at framhaldsskóli age. It runs two years (four semesters) and centres on Icelandic at **A1–B1**. It started autumn 2024. — [Kvennaskólinn: Íslenskubraut](https://www.kvenno.is/is/nam-kennsla/namsbrautir-1/islenskubraut-nytt)
- Kvennaskólinn's progression rules: to enter year 2, students must pass four first-year courses, **ÍSAN1ÍA10, ÍSAN1ÍD10, ÍSAN1ÍC05 and ÍSAN1ÍF05**. The rules describe the brautin as focused on Icelandic "á 1. og 2. þrepi (A1–B1)". — [Kvennaskólinn: Reglur um námsframvindu](https://www.kvenno.is/is/nam-kennsla/namsmat-og-namsframvinda/reglur-um-namsframvindu)
- Kvennaskólinn intake: 15 students admitted in the most recent intake, with about 30 expected on the brautin in the autumn. The announcement's exact year was not visible in the summary. — [Kvennaskólinn: Innritun lokið](https://www.kvenno.is/is/frettir/innritun-lokid-3)
- **FMOS** frames its brautin for students whose first language is not Icelandic and who have varying foundations in their own language, literacy and study. One end goal is further study in Icelandic framhaldsskólar. — [FMOS: Íslenskubraut](https://www.fmos.is/is/namid/brautir/islenskubraut); MÍ: [Menntaskólinn á Ísafirði: Íslenskubraut](https://misa.snerpill.is/namid/islenskubraut/)
- The national programme register has an "Íslenskubraut fyrir útlendinga" open to any student of foreign origin at upper-secondary age. It "veitir ekki eiginleg réttindi" (gives no formal qualification). A search summary reports such brautir at about 115 credits over four semesters. That figure was not seen on the page itself. — [namskra.is: Íslenskubraut fyrir útlendinga](https://namskra.is/programmes/014c0d3b-feb1-4d5f-9a28-5573fc2f6b53)
- **Akureyri:** VMA set **B1** as the Icelandic benchmark for admitting students of foreign origin to regular programmes. Its "Íslenskubrú" (launched at VMA in spring 2024) moved to Menntaskólinn á Akureyri in autumn 2025. The sources say students below B1 often find framhaldsskóli study "ofviða" (beyond them). — [VMA: B1 … viðmið við inntöku](https://www.vma.is/is/skolinn/frettir/b1-a-evropska-tungumalarammanum-er-vidmid-vid-inntoku-nemenda-af-erlendum-uppruna); [MA: Um skólann](https://www.ma.is/is/skolinn/skolinn-og-starfid/um-menntaskolann-a-akuryeyri)
- Standalone ÍSAN courses are common, for example **ÍSAN1AA06** "Íslenska sem annað mál. Fyrir byrjendur" at MH. Codes such as ÍSAN2GM05 also appear in school catalogues. — [MH: ÍSAN1AA06](https://www.mh.is/is/namid/skipulag-nams/yfirlit-afanga/index/course/isan1aa06-islenska-sem-annad-mal); [FSS: Áfangar í boði](https://www.fss.is/is/onnin/val/afangar-i-bodi-leidbeiningar); [Næsta skref: Íslenska sem annað mál](https://naestaskref.is/is/nam/islenska-sem-annad-mal)
- MEMM's October 2025 interim report reviewed the íslenskubrautir after their first year, using a questionnaire to those involved. The agreement with the schools was extended to the end of 2026. The report recommends standardised assessment of students of foreign origin at all school levels and more targeted reception plans. It also reports that a framhaldsskóli specialist joined the ministry's framhaldsskólateymi in May 2025. — [Þróunarverkefnið MEMM: Áfangaskýrsla, Oct 2025](<https://www.stjornarradid.is/library/02-Rit-skyrslur-og-skrar/MRN/afangaskyrsla-MEMM-24-25%20-%20Copy%20(1).pdf>); [Stjórnarráðið listing, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.stjornarradid.is/gogn/rit-og-skyrslur/stakt-rit/2025/10/30/Throunarverkefnid-MEMM-menntun-mottaka-menning-Afangaskyrsla-/)
- The Menntastefna 2030 second action plan (2025–2027, July 2025) includes measure 7: strengthen support for, and follow-up of, Icelandic teaching for children and young people with diverse language and cultural backgrounds. It also covers increasing the number of ISL-specialised teachers and making core-subject materials in framhaldsskóli (Icelandic, English, maths) free of charge. — [Menntastefna 2030 – Önnur aðgerðaáætlun 2025–2027](https://www.stjornarradid.is/library/02-Rit-skyrslur-og-skrar/MRN/Menntastefna-2030-Onnur-adgerdaaaetlun-2025-2027.pdf); critical commentary: [Skólaþræðir, 7 Sept 2025: Safn sundurleitra aðgerða](https://skolathraedir.is/2025/09/07/safn-sundurleitra-adgerda-onnur-adgerdaaaetlun-i-menntamalum/)
- The language-policy action plan 2024–2026 asks for a coordinated procedure (samræmt verklag) for reception, teaching and services for children with diverse language backgrounds, with a special emphasis on ISL. — [Þingsályktun 582/154: aðgerðaáætlun í málefnum íslenskrar tungu 2024–2026](https://www.althingi.is/altext/154/s/0582.html)

### Inferences

- **CEFR alignment:** The three íslenskubrautir state their target as A1–B1, which matches the MMS grunnskóli framework's 1.–3. stig exactly. Kvenno.app's A1/A2/B1 card levels therefore already sit on the right scale. Labelling cards with both the CEFR level and "1./2./3. stig" would let teachers from either system read them.
- **The age-independent framework is reusable:** No ISL competence framework exists at framhaldsskóli level, and the MMS framework is explicitly not tied to age. Its descriptors are therefore the most defensible source of learning outcomes a small tool can cite. Kvennaskólinn's own ÍSAN1Í* course descriptions are the local authority.
- **A tool can serve a whole cohort, not only Kvenno:** "Íslenskubraut" is a new, ministry-backed programme type with three pilot schools sharing a MEMM support network and extended to 2026. The schools are explicitly looking for materials, so a free tool built at one of the three could be shared through MEMM.
- **B1 is the threshold that matters:** B1 is the admission benchmark for regular programmes (VMA) and the stated exit level of the brautir. The cards' B1 tier, and anything bridging into subject-specific (academic) vocabulary, is where the stakes are highest.

## 2. What Icelandic research says about immigrant students: dropout, PISA, the OECD reviews and named researchers

### Takeaway

Immigrant students drop out of framhaldsskóli at roughly 2.5 times the rate of students without a foreign background: about 46 % against about 18 % for the autumn-2016 cohort. They also trail by about 60–74 PISA points in reading, and PISA 2025 (published 8 September 2026) reportedly found only about 28 % of students with a foreign background reaching baseline reading proficiency. The OECD's 2023–2025 reports keep returning to two problems: under-specified language teaching and the absence of ISL teacher qualifications. No standalone "OECD 2021/2022 review of education for immigrant students in Iceland" was found. The relevant OECD document is the 2024 _Skills and Labour Market Integration of Immigrants and their Children in Iceland_.

### Cited Findings

**Dropout and completion**

- Statistics Iceland reports that just over 46 % of immigrants who started day-school upper-secondary study in autumn 2016 had left without graduating four years later. The comparable figure was just under 18 % for students with no foreign background and just over 13 % for those born abroad with an Icelandic background. — [Hagstofa: Brautskráningarhlutfall og brottfall af framhaldsskólastigi 2020](https://hagstofa.is/utgafur/frettasafn/menntun/brautskraningarhlutfall-og-brottfall-af-framhaldsskolastigi-2020)
- Of the 320 immigrants who started day study in autumn 2015, nearly 36 % had graduated by 2019. — [Hagstofa: … 2019](https://hagstofa.is/utgafur/frettasafn/menntun/brautskraningarhlutfall-og-brotthvarf-af-framhaldsskolastigi-2019/)
- An older figure: a 2015 study cited in a parliamentary answer found only 22 % of foreign-background students in the 2010 cohort had graduated. — [Svar 880/146 um brottfall nemenda af erlendum uppruna](https://www.althingi.is/altext/146/s/0880.html)
- Attendance gap: at age 19, about 7 in 10 native residents attended framhaldsskóli against about 2 in 10 immigrants (2008–2017 data). — [Hagstofa: Félagsvísar – sérhefti um innflytjendur](https://www.hagstofa.is/utgafur/frettasafn/lifskjor/felagsvisar-serhefti-um-innflytjendur/)
- Caveat: Hagstofa counts first-time registrants as nýnemar, so foreign exchange students count as new students. This can overstate immigrant dropout, and immigrants' wider age spread also skews the comparison. — [Stjórnarráðið: Félagsleg og efnahagsleg staða og brotthvarf úr íslenskum framhaldsskólum (Jan 2022)](https://www.stjornarradid.is/library/02-Rit--skyrslur-og-skrar/Sk%C3%BDsla%20-%20brotthvarf%20%C3%BAr%20framhaldssk%C3%B3lum%2024.01.2022.pdf)
- A Netla article (2024) covering 2001–2021 finds that people born abroad are increasingly likely to start framhaldsskóli and proportionally more now finish. Male dropout is markedly higher than female dropout regardless of background. — [Netla: Alþjóðlegur bakgrunnur, skólasókn og brotthvarf úr íslenskum framhaldsskólum 2001–2021](https://ojs.hi.is/index.php/netla/article/view/3608)
- Iceland's overall early-leaving rate (18–24-year-olds with at most lower-secondary education, not in education or training) was the highest in Europe in 2022, at 16 % against an average of 9 %. The OECD names language proficiency as a likely factor in whether children of migrants progress. — [OECD 2024, ch. "Transmitting skills to children of immigrants"](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/skills-and-labour-market-integration-of-immigrants-and-their-children-in-iceland_96adc300-en/full-report/component-8.html)
- A BA thesis (Skemman) on dropout of foreign-origin students from framhaldsskóli exists. — [Skemman 1946/48465](https://skemman.is/bitstream/1946/48465/1/BA-%20ritger%C3%B0%20-SKIL.pdf)
- A Netla study of "fragile educational opportunities" at the end of grunnskóli describes foreign-origin students compromising on their future hopes. — [Netla: Brothættir menntunarmöguleikar nemenda af erlendum uppruna við lok grunnskóla](https://ejournals.is/index.php/netla/article/view/3959)

**PISA**

- PISA 2022: 236 students in Iceland, 7.4 % of respondents, were classified as immigrants, and 58 students were exempted for limited Icelandic. — [Reykjavíkurborg: PISA 2022 – helsta Ísland](https://reykjavik.is/sites/default/files/2023-12/pisa_2022_helsta_island.pdf)
- Size of the reading gap: a parliamentary report gives the reading-literacy gap as −73.8 points, and −50.2 after adjusting for background. The year the figures refer to was not visible in the summary. — [Þingskjal 625, 154. löggjafarþing](https://www.althingi.is/altext/pdf/154/s/0625.pdf). Morgunblaðið reports a gap of "over 60 points" in reading. — [mbl.is, 10 Sept 2026: Brotalöm í kennslu erlendra barna](https://www.mbl.is/frettir/innlent/2026/09/10/brotalom_i_kennslu_erlendra_barna/). The sources differ in size and likely in the year measured; quote them with years.
- **PISA 2025** (published 8 September 2026): Iceland scored 422 in reading against an OECD average of 461, down 14 points since 2022. 56 % of students reached baseline reading proficiency. — [Menntavísindastofnun HÍ: PISA 2025](https://menntavisindastofnun.hi.is/is/pisa-2025); [OECD PISA 2025 Results Vol. I, Iceland country note](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2025-results-volume-i-country-notes_2d4ff9ea-en/iceland_1ca66d16-en.html); [mbl.is: Ísland hrunið mest allra ríkja í PISA](https://www.mbl.is/frettir/innlent/2026/09/08/island_hrunid_mest_allra_rikja_i_pisa/)
- Vísir reports that only about **28 %** of students with a foreign background reach basic reading proficiency in PISA 2025. The gap shrinks but does not disappear when socio-economic status is controlled for. This was not confirmed against the official report. — [Vísir: Aðeins um 28 prósent innflytjenda með grunnfærni í lesskilningi](https://www.visir.is/g/20262931952d/adeins-um-28-prosent-innflytjenda-med-grunnfaerni-i-lesskilningi); [mbl.is: Bilið breikkar mest á Íslandi](https://www.mbl.is/frettir/innlent/2026/09/08/bilid_breikkar_mest_a_islandi/)
- The OECD Economic Survey 2025 says more than half of immigrant students lack the reading skills needed for further education or work. It recommends well-targeted, systematic language training. The 2023 survey also noted wide underperformance in reading and higher upper-secondary dropout. — [OECD Economic Surveys: Iceland 2025, "Raising foundational skills"](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-iceland-2025_890dbe05-en/full-report/raising-foundational-skills-and-education-quality_7c04602b.html); [OECD Economic Surveys: Iceland 2023](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-iceland-2023_b3880f1a-en.html)

**OECD on ISL teaching**

- The OECD (2024) notes there are no qualification requirements for language teachers, so outcomes "may vary greatly". It recommends formal requirements and funded teacher preparation. A Master's in Second Language Teaching launched in 2016 has low enrolment. The OECD urges quality standards for Icelandic teaching and alignment of curricula with the CEFR, as in the other Nordic countries. — [OECD 2024 report (PDF)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/09/skills-and-labour-market-integration-of-immigrants-and-their-children-in-iceland_cc5aa7de/96adc300-en.pdf); [OECD 2024, ch. "Developing and assessing skills for integration"](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/skills-and-labour-market-integration-of-immigrants-and-their-children-in-iceland_96adc300-en/full-report/component-6.html)
- An OECD Economics working paper on immigration in Iceland (2023) also exists. — [OECD ECO WP No. 1772](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/11/immigration-in-iceland_e1b2c815/645ca1ac-en.pdf)

- Anna Katarzyna Wozniczka and Kriselle Lou Suson Jónsdóttir's co-authored work found is on **university** students of foreign origin ("Kennsla og stuðningur í íslenskum háskólum: Reynsla innflytjenda", 2018), not framhaldsskóli. Kriselle Suson Jónsdóttir now works in Reykjavík's education department on the reception of children of foreign origin. Brynja E. Halldórsdóttir's school-related piece is a chapter ("Bara ekki mínar týpur!") on identity and choosing a secondary school. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/37902142/Kennsla_og_stu%C3%B0ningur_%C3%AD_%C3%ADslenskum_h%C3%A1sk%C3%B3lum_Reynsla_innflytjenda); [Reykjavík staff page](https://reykjavik.is/en/department-of-education-and-youth-staff)
- A broader edited volume: "Menntun og þátttaka í nýju landi: Reynsla innflytjenda, flóttafólks og skóla". — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338865713_Menntun_og_thatttaka_i_nyju_landi_Reynsla_innflytjenda_flottafolks_og_skola)

### Inferences

- The target group, 16–20-year-olds arriving with little Icelandic, is the highest-risk segment. They enter a system where completing the standard route is roughly a coin-flip for immigrants (about 46 % dropout). Reading in Icelandic is the bottleneck named by PISA and the OECD alike.
- The research base on _framhaldsskóli specifically_ is thinner than on grunnskóli. Most named researchers' recent work is on compulsory school, universities or transitions. A tool's design rationale will lean on grunnskóli and OECD sources plus the three íslenskubrautir' own experience.

### Gaps

- The search did not surface a separate "OECD 2021/2022 review of education for immigrant students in Iceland". If the coordinator has a specific title, it could not be confirmed. The OECD _Strength through Diversity_ country reviews did not appear for Iceland.
- No Hagstofa breakdown by background was found for cohorts after autumn 2016.
- The PISA 2025 immigrant figures (28 %) were confirmed only from press coverage, not the OECD tables.

## 3. Gaps and needs the sources name, including what teachers of these students report needing

### Takeaway

The recurring needs are: assessment tools tied to the CEFR; materials pitched at teenagers between A2 and B1 and into academic Icelandic; trained ISL teachers and teacher support; coordinated reception; and attention to academic vocabulary. Upper-secondary-specific teacher voices are sparse in what could be retrieved. The 2025 Netla article that surfaced is about COVID, not ISL.

### Cited Findings

- The MEMM interim report (Oct 2025) names a need for "mat og verkfæri til að styðja við inngildingu, íslensku sem annað mál og fjöltyngi" (assessment and tools to support inclusion, ISL and multilingualism). It recommends standardised assessment at all school levels and better-targeted reception plans. — [MEMM áfangaskýrsla, Oct 2025](<https://www.stjornarradid.is/library/02-Rit-skyrslur-og-skrar/MRN/afangaskyrsla-MEMM-24-25%20-%20Copy%20(1).pdf>)
- The Menntastefna action plan 2025–2027 aims to increase the number of teachers specialised in ISL and to strengthen follow-up of Icelandic teaching. — [Menntastefna 2030 – Önnur aðgerðaáætlun](https://www.stjornarradid.is/library/02-Rit-skyrslur-og-skrar/MRN/Menntastefna-2030-Onnur-adgerdaaaetlun-2025-2027.pdf)
- The OECD flags the lack of language-teacher qualification requirements, the need for quality standards, and the need for CEFR alignment. — [OECD 2024](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/09/skills-and-labour-market-integration-of-immigrants-and-their-children-in-iceland_cc5aa7de/96adc300-en.pdf)
- In September 2026 the minister was quoted saying specific measures are needed, especially training teachers to teach Icelandic to children who speak it as an additional language. — [mbl.is, 10 Sept 2026](https://www.mbl.is/frettir/innlent/2026/09/10/brotalom_i_kennslu_erlendra_barna/)
- In a 2017 interview study of 38 compulsory-school teachers, teachers felt they were not well enough supported to understand and meet these students' learning needs ("kennarar telja sig ekki vera nægilega vel studda"). — [Tímarit um uppeldi og menntun 26(1–2) 2017](https://ejournals.is/index.php/tuuom/article/download/2685/1447/3769); see also [Nemendur af erlendum uppruna: Reynsla foreldra og kennara](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323961410_Nemendur_af_erlendum_uppruna_Reynsla_foreldra_og_kennara_af_nami_og_kennslu)
- A framhaldsskóli teacher talk at MA (2018), "Nemendur með íslensku sem annað tungumál (ÍSAT) – Hvaða orð? Hvernig texti?", is framed around which words to teach and which texts to use. It points to vocabulary selection and text level as practitioners' working questions. — [MA haustnám 2018, Hulda Karen Daníelsdóttir](https://www.msha.is/static/files/haustnam18/hulda-karen-danielsdottir.pdf)
- A master's thesis states that, until LÍNO, Icelandic had no detailed frequency word list of academic vocabulary comparable to English's NAWL. This is an explicit gap in academic-vocabulary resources. — [Skemman: Listi yfir íslenskan námsorðaforða](https://skemman.is/bitstream/1946/42393/1/A%CC%81sdi%CC%81s%20Bjo%CC%88rg%20Bjo%CC%88rgvinsdo%CC%81ttir-meistaraverkefni.pdf)
- An older M.Paed. thesis (Solveig Brynja Grétarsdóttir, 2007) tracked the progress of 119 framhaldsskóli students with a non-Icelandic mother tongue. It is cited in the 2013 Netla article. — [Tungumálatorg listing](https://tungumalatorg.is/isa/fr%C3%A6%C3%B0sla/flokkur/)
- The 2025 Netla article on framhaldsskóli teachers (Guðrún Ragnarsdóttir, Elsa Eiríksdóttir, Súsanna Margrét Gestsdóttir, Amalía Björnsdóttir) concerns COVID-era learning loss. Its only ISL content is a literature-review statement that students with another first language are much less likely to finish framhaldsskóli. — [Netla, 16 May 2025](https://ejournals.is/index.php/netla/article/download/4167/2777/7768)
- MEMM's own materials page lists only a modest set of ISL titles for teenagers (see §4). — [memm.mms.is: Námsefni](https://memm.mms.is/menntun/namsefni)

### Inferences

- The gaps a small web tool can realistically address are:
  - leveled, teen-appropriate speaking and description practice at A1–B1, which the cards already target;
  - a controlled path into **subject/academic vocabulary** (LÍNO-2), for students heading into regular programmes;
  - audio models of pronunciation via TTS;
  - teacher-facing, CEFR-labelled materials that lower preparation time.
- The tool should not try to provide assessment or certification, which is MEMM's and the national stöðumat's domain. It could, however, label everything with CEFR / 1.–3. stig so teachers can map it onto whatever assessment emerges.

### Gaps

- No Skemman thesis or Ráðstefnurit Netlu paper was found that directly reports framhaldsskóli ISL teachers' material needs. Skemman full-text search for "íslenska sem annað mál" + "framhaldsskóli" is needed, but the site was unreachable.
- No Skólaþræðir article specifically on ISL in framhaldsskóli was surfaced. Only the action-plan critique was found.

## 4. Existing free materials and tools for learning Icelandic as an L2: what they do, quality, target group, licence/API

### Takeaway

Free self-study for adults is reasonably covered (Icelandic Online, IceFlash 4K, Árnastofnun dictionaries). There are school workbooks for grunnskóli, and a few MMS titles reach framhaldsskóli. What appears to be missing is free, teacher-led, **teenager-oriented, topic-based speaking material at A1–B1 with built-in morphology and audio**. That is the niche the Íslenskubraut cards and sentence builder occupy. None of the learner products found offers an open API. The open data lives at Árnastofnun and CLARIN-IS (§5).

### Cited Findings

**Icelandic Online** (University of Iceland / Árnastofnun)

- Free, self-directed distance courses in Icelandic as a foreign language: the survival course **Bjargir** (Pre-A1, aimed at people living and working in Iceland), then IO 1–5 spanning roughly A1–C1. Each course is about 45–90 hours. The paid **Icelandic Online PLUS** adds an 8-week tutor-supported course. Sources disagree on the number of courses (five or six) and the exact CEFR mapping. — [Árnastofnun: About Icelandic Online](https://www.arnastofnun.is/en/about-icelandic-online); [icelandiconline.com/about](https://icelandiconline.com/about); [Where to learn Icelandic: online courses](https://wheretolearnicelandic.org/docs/classes-and-courses/online-courses/)
- A third-party review rates it "Good, but lacks explanations". — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/icelandic-online/)
- Licence/API: none found. It is a closed web platform.

**Bara tala**

- An AI-driven app that listens to users speak, helps with pronunciation and gives real-time feedback. It is free to download, but full access (over 140 hours of content) is a paid licence, often sponsored by unions or employers (BHM, FÍN). It is used, for example, by foreign preschool staff in Hafnarfjörður. The target is working adults and workplace Icelandic. — [baratala.is](https://www.baratala.is/en); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/vg/app/bara-tala/id6450674601); [BHM](https://www.bhm.is/vidburdir/bara-tala-learn-and-practice-icelandic); [Hafnarfjörður](https://en.hafnarfjordur.is/the-bara-tala-app-empowers-foreign-preschool-staff/)
- Licence/API: proprietary. No API found.

**Íslenskuþorpið (the Icelandic Village)**

- A University of Iceland teaching project based on Guðrún Theodórsdóttir's doctoral research (2010, conversation analysis). Learners take part in real everyday interactions in partner businesses and institutions, using deliberate strategies, and debrief in class. — [EPALE: Eldri borgarar kenna góða íslensku](https://epale.ec.europa.eu/en/node/29154); article: Guðrún Theodórsdóttir & Kolbrún Friðriksdóttir, "Íslenskuþorpið: Leið til þátttöku í daglegum samskiptum á íslensku", _Milli mála_ 2013 (cited via the EPALE summary)
- No source linked it to framhaldsskóli. It is a method, not a material or a tool.

**MMS / Miðstöð menntunar og skólaþjónustu materials (námsefni)**

- The memm.mms.is materials page lists **Íslenska fyrir alla 1–4** (A1, with some higher-level material) and **Hvítar lygar** (for framhaldsskóli students at about A2/B1). — [memm.mms.is: Námsefni](https://memm.mms.is/menntun/namsefni)
- **Einmitt 1a–1c** (1. stig, aimed at unglingastig, usable at miðstig), _Kæra dagbók_ and others are on the ÍSAT list. — [memm.mms.is: ÍSAT námsefni](https://memm.mms.is/menntun/isat-namsefni)
- **Málfræðibókin mín 1–3** (María Garðarsdóttir, Sigríður Þorvaldsdóttir) are grammar workbooks for students with another first language who already have fairly good Icelandic. They are mainly for unglingastig and also tagged for framhaldsskóli. — [mms.is: Málfræðibókin mín 1](https://mms.is/namsefni/malfraedibokin-min-1-hefti)
- **Fræðslugátt** hosts ISL resources, including "Íslenska – Tölum saman", described as suitable for the upper grades of grunnskóli, framhaldsskóli and adults. **Tungumálatorg** (tungumalatorg.is/isa) hosts ISL teaching resources and articles. — [Fræðslugátt: Íslenska sem annað tungumál](https://fraedslugatt.is/annad-efni/isat/); [Tungumálatorg](https://tungumalatorg.is/isa/flokkur-2/islenskan/1-2-or%C3%B0flokkarnir/)
- Licence/API: MMS materials are free to schools, as print and PDF/web. No open licence or API was found.

**Vocabulary apps**

- **IceFlash 4K** (University of Iceland researchers and students): a free flashcard app (mobile and web) with the **4,000 most frequent Icelandic words**. Frequencies are from the Icelandic Gold Standard corpus (MÍM-GOLD), and translations into English, Polish, Chinese and Ukrainian were made by hand. Aimed at L2 learners. — [HÍ: Nýtt orðaforðaapp fyrir fólk sem er að læra íslensku](https://hi.is/frettir/nytt_ordafordaapp_fyrir_folk_sem_er_ad_laera_islensku); [Iceland Review](https://www.icelandreview.com/news/culture/new-app-for-learning-icelandic-vocabulary/)
- Commercial apps (Drops, Memrise, Tobo, "Learn Icelandic Vocabulary") exist with free tiers. They teach vocabulary only, not grammar, and roundups ranking them are often affiliate-driven. — [Drops (Google Play)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.languagedrops.drops.learn.learning.speak.language.icelandic.words&hl=en_US); [Tobo (App Store)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tobo-learn-icelandic-words/id1520464225)

**Dictionaries (Árnastofnun)**

- **Íslensk-pólsk orðabók / Słownik islandzko-polski**: free online, about 54,000–54,600 Icelandic headwords with Polish translations. Search runs in both directions, headwords have **audio pronunciation**, inflections link to **BÍN**, and some entries are illustrated. It is aimed at the Polish-speaking community in Iceland, the largest immigrant group. — [polska.arnastofnun.is: Um](https://polska.arnastofnun.is/is/um/); [polska.arnastofnun.is](https://polska.arnastofnun.is/)
- **ISLEX**: a multilingual online dictionary from Icelandic to Danish, Swedish, Norwegian (Bokmål and Nynorsk), Faroese and Finnish. **LEXÍA**: Icelandic–French/German, built on ISLEX and still being developed. — [ISLEX](https://islex.arnastofnun.is/is/leit/%C3%8Dsland); [LEXIA](https://lexia.arnastofnun.is/)
- **Íslensk-ensk orðabók** at enska.arnastofnun.is. — [Íslensk-ensk orðabók](https://enska.arnastofnun.is/is/leit/rakakrem?vefir_valmynd_onnur_sofn=islenskordabok.arnastofnun.is)
- **Íslensk nútímamálsorðabók**: its editorial policy explicitly keeps learners of Icelandic as a second language in mind. — [islenskordabok.arnastofnun.is: stefna](https://islenskordabok.arnastofnun.is/stefna/)
- **Málið.is**: a single portal searching Árnastofnun's datasets (dictionaries, BÍN, spelling and others) in one place. — search summary of [polska.arnastofnun.is](https://polska.arnastofnun.is/is/about/)
- Licence/API: the dictionaries are free to use online. No open licence or public API for ISLEX, the Polish dictionary or Málið was found in this session. **BÍN** is the exception (see §5).

**Not found**

- "Learn Icelandic / Gagnvirk", "Orðaforði / Orðasjóður" and "Fleiri orð?" did not surface in any search. They may not exist under those names.

### Inferences

- Of the existing materials, the closest to the Íslenskubraut cards are MMS's _Hvítar lygar_ and _Íslenska fyrir alla_. Both are print-style readers and workbooks, not picture-description speaking frames with inflection support. Overlap looks low, but the A1 vocabulary of _Íslenska fyrir alla_ is worth cross-checking against the six card topics.
- Two dictionaries matter for the target group. The **Polish dictionary**, with audio and BÍN links, is the best existing bilingual resource for the largest L1 group. **IceFlash 4K** (Polish, Ukrainian, Chinese and English glosses) is the best existing free frequency-based vocabulary tool. Linking out to them is better than duplicating them.
- Icelandic Online and Bara tala target adults and workplace Icelandic. Neither is framed for teenagers in a school setting, and Bara tala is paywalled.

### Gaps

- No quality evaluations of MMS ISL materials for framhaldsskóli were found.
- Licence terms of the Árnastofnun bilingual dictionaries could not be read (site unreachable).
- No up-to-date list of what the three íslenskubrautir actually use in class was found.

## 5. Free Icelandic language-technology resources a small school web tool could build on (licences, APIs, open data)

### Takeaway

There is an unusually rich open stack, much of it from the government Language Technology Programme (Máltækniáætlun, run by Almannarómur). The defaults are **CC BY 4.0 for data and models and Apache-2.0 for code**, unless a repository states otherwise. The pieces most useful to Íslenskubraut are: BÍN, already used (CC BY-SA 4.0); Tiro TTS with the Diljá and Álfur voices (open web service with an OpenAPI); LÍNO-2 academic word list and MÍNO frequency list (CLARIN-IS); IceFlash-style frequency from MÍM-GOLD; Icegrams (MIT); and GreynirCorrect, the engine behind Yfirlestur.is (MIT). Risamálheild is **mixed-licence**, so check which sub-corpora a frequency list is derived from.

### Cited Findings

**Programme defaults**

- The Language Technology Programme's documentation states that, unless a project's repository says otherwise, datasets and models are CC-BY-4.0 and source code is Apache-2.0. The plan's core deliverables were open speech recognition, speech synthesis, machine translation and a proofreader. — [icelandic-lt/doc/tts.md](https://github.com/icelandic-lt/icelandic-lt/blob/main/doc/tts.md); [icelandic-lt/doc/lr.md](https://github.com/icelandic-lt/icelandic-lt/blob/main/doc/lr.md); [Vísir 2021: Álfur og Diljá hefja upp raust sína](https://www.visir.is/g/20212183967d/alfur-og-dilja-hefja-upp-raust-sina)

**Text-to-speech**

- **Diljá** (female) and **Álfur** (male) were developed at Reykjavík University within the programme. 48 human voices were recorded for the work. The v2 voices use ESPnet2 FastSpeech2 with a Multiband MelGAN vocoder. — [Vísir 2021](https://www.visir.is/g/20212183967d/alfur-og-dilja-hefja-upp-raust-sina); [GitHub: tiro-is/tiro-tts](https://github.com/tiro-is/tiro-tts)
- **Tiro TTS web service**: takes plain text or SSML and returns MP3, Ogg Vorbis or 16-bit PCM, or **speech marks with word timing**. OpenAPI documentation is at tts.tiro.is, where about four voices are available. The code is open and also deposited on CLARIN-IS. The terms of use and rate limits for the public endpoint were **not** found. — [GitHub: tiro-tts](https://github.com/tiro-is/tiro-tts); [CLARIN-IS: Tiro TTS web service 22.10](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12537/268); [Borealium: Tiro](https://borealium.org/is/resource/tiro/)
- **Símarómur** (Grammatek): an Android TTS engine, Apache-licensed code. It offers network voices via the Tiro API plus on-device voices; the on-device **Steinn** voice model is **CC-BY-4.0**. — [GitHub: grammatek/simaromur](https://github.com/grammatek/simaromur); [Grammatek: Símarómur](https://www.grammatek.com/en/simaromur); [CLARIN-IS: Icelandic TTS for Android 24.04](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12537/328?locale-attribute=is)
- **Guðrún**, an Icelandic neural voice in Microsoft Edge's read-aloud, came from a collaboration between Almannarómur and the SÍM group. In effect it is free in-browser TTS for students on Edge. — [Vísir 2022: Guðrún hjálpar Microsoft Edge að tala íslensku](https://www.visir.is/g/20222221218d/gudrun-hjalpar-microsoft-edge-ad-tala-islensku)
- **icespeak** (Miðeind, PyPI v0.4.0) is a **GPL-3.0** Python wrapper whose voices come from Azure and AWS Polly. It needs cloud API keys, so it is not a free voice source. — read directly from [PyPI: icespeak](https://pypi.org/project/icespeak/)

**Speech recognition**

- The **Samrómur** crowd-sourced speech corpus is CC-BY 4.0. There is also a Samrómur Children corpus. The Kaldi recipes on GitHub contain no trained models. — [GitHub: icelandic-lt/samromur-asr](https://github.com/icelandic-lt/samromur-asr); [LREC 2022: Samrómur Children](https://aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.105.pdf)
- RU's Language and Voice Lab has published **Whisper models fine-tuned for Icelandic** (about 1,000 hours, including Samrómur and Málrómur) on Hugging Face. — [HF: language-and-voice-lab/whisper-large-icelandic-30k-steps-1000h](https://huggingface.co/language-and-voice-lab/whisper-large-icelandic-30k-steps-1000h)

**Morphology, parsing, correction (Miðeind; licences read directly from PyPI on 2026-10-16)**

- **islenska** v2.1.0 (BinPackage): BÍN as a Python package. The code is **MIT**; the package description states the BÍN data itself is released under **CC BY-SA 4.0**. — [PyPI: islenska](https://pypi.org/project/islenska/)
- **reynir-correct** v4.2.0 (**GreynirCorrect**, "Spelling and grammar correction for Icelandic"), © 2025 Miðeind, **MIT**. This is the engine behind Yfirlestur.is. A small tool could self-host it server-side. — [PyPI: reynir-correct](https://pypi.org/project/reynir-correct/)
- **reynir** v3.9.0 (Greynir parser), **tokenizer** v3.7.0 and **icegrams** v2.0.0. Icegrams is MIT and holds over 78 million trigrams and about 1.7 million unigrams, built from **Risamálheild** (IGC) plus recent news, so it offers word-form frequency lookups. — [PyPI: icegrams](https://pypi.org/project/icegrams/); [PyPI: reynir](https://pypi.org/project/reynir/)
- Community index of Icelandic NLP resources. — [cadia-lvl: Icelandic NLP resources](https://cadia-lvl.github.io/icelandic-NLP-resources/)

**Corpora and frequency**

- **Risamálheild / Icelandic Gigaword Corpus (IGC)**: distributed via CLARIN-IS (IGC-2022 annotated; IGC-2024ext unannotated). It is **mixed-licence**: parts are CC BY, and parts carry a special MÍM-derived licence. Under that licence, results can be used freely, but extracts may not be published or exploited commercially beyond stated exceptions. A third-party registry lists it simply as CC BY 4.0, which conflicts; check the per-sub-corpus licence files. — [CLARIN-IS: IGC licence](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/page/license-gigaword-corpus); [IGC Part 1 licence](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/page/license-gigaword-corpus-p1); [IGC-2022 annotated](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12537/254); [IGC-2024ext](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12537/359); [igc.arnastofnun.is](https://igc.arnastofnun.is/)
- Historic frequency source: _Íslensk orðtíðnibók_ (1991). Eiríkur Rögnvaldsson compares the top word forms across the 1940 count, the 1991 book and a spoken corpus, and finds near-identical sets in different orders. — [Eiríkur Rögnvaldsson: Algengustu orðmyndir málsins](https://uni.hi.is/eirikur/2020/11/28/algengustu-ordmyndir-malsins/)

### Inferences

- **TTS is the highest-value, lowest-cost addition.** Tiro's speech marks with word timing would allow word-by-word highlighting of a generated sentence. This is ideal for the sentence builder and for the oral picture-description cards. Pre-rendering audio at build time, as the repo already does for PDFs, avoids depending on the endpoint's undocumented rate limits. The voice models' CC-BY-4.0 terms (where stated) require attribution, alongside the BÍN (CC BY-SA) and ARASAAC (CC BY-NC-SA) attributions the app already carries.
- **GreynirCorrect (MIT)** could give feedback on short free-text B1 answers. It is designed for native-speaker text, though, so its feedback on learner errors is untested here. Treat it as an experiment, not a grader.
- Frequency data for choosing or validating card vocabulary should come from a source with a clear licence: the MÍNO frequency list, MÍM-GOLD (via IceFlash's approach) or Icegrams (MIT). Raw Risamálheild extracts are a poor choice because of the MÍM licence restriction.
- **ASR** (the fine-tuned Whisper models) is technically available for speaking practice, but running it needs a GPU server or a paid host. That is likely out of scope for a small school tool.

### Gaps

- The licence and terms of the public **tts.tiro.is** endpoint (free use? rate limits? attribution?), and the licence of the Diljá and Álfur models, could not be confirmed. GitHub and CLARIN were unreachable from this session. The Tiro README notes the Diljá models were "yet to be published" at one point.
- Whether **Yfirlestur.is** offers a public API for third parties was not confirmed. The search returned nothing about Yfirlestur.
- No Icelandic machine-translation service with confirmed open licence and API terms (e.g. Miðeind's Vélþýðing) was checked.
- No evidence was found of an "Íslenskur grunnorðaforði" / "Icelandic Common Core Vocabulary" list tied to CEFR levels (see §6).

## 6. Is there a recognised Icelandic academic word list, or lists of frequent words for school subjects?

### Takeaway

Yes, for academic vocabulary. **LÍNO-2, the Icelandic Academic Word List** (Auður Pálsdóttir and Sigríður Ólafsdóttir, HÍ), and its source corpus MÍNO are openly deposited on CLARIN-IS. LÍNO v0.9 had 1,515 lemmas used across disciplines beyond the most common words. A MÍNO frequency list (10,313 words) also exists. **No** CEFR-graded "core vocabulary" list for Icelandic as an L2 was found. The nearest things are IceFlash 4K's 4,000 most frequent words (MÍM-GOLD) and the old _Íslensk orðtíðnibók_.

### Cited Findings

- **LÍNO-2 (The Icelandic Academic Word List v1.0)**, by Auður Pálsdóttir and Sigríður Ólafsdóttir (HÍ, Deild faggreinakennslu), was made available on 4 January 2023 via CLARIN-IS. — [IRIS: The Icelandic Academic Word List (v1.0) LÍNO-2](https://iris.landsbokasafn.is/en/datasets/the-icelandic-academic-word-list-v-10-lino-2/)
- **LÍNO v0.9**: 1,515 lemmas "beyond the most common words … used across disciplines". - **MÍNO, the Corpus of Icelandic Academic Vocabulary** (v1.0): selected academic texts from MÍM (the Tagged Icelandic Corpus) and the IGC. — [CLARIN-IS: MÍNO v1.0](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12537/299); [MÍNO 0.9](https://repository.clarin.is/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12537/235)

### Inferences

- LÍNO-2 is the natural basis for a **B1→B2 "bridge to subject study"** extension, for example a "námsorð" layer of cards. It is the recognised list, it is HÍ-authored and it is openly deposited. Pairing each LÍNO lemma with BÍN inflections, which the app already uses, and with Tiro audio would be new; nothing found does this.
- A CEFR-graded core vocabulary list does not appear to exist. The card vocabulary should therefore be validated by frequency (IceFlash/MÍM-GOLD or Icegrams) plus teacher judgement, rather than claimed to follow an official list.
- No subject-specific lists for school subjects (e.g. chemistry, history) were found. LÍNO is cross-disciplinary by design.

### Gaps

- LÍNO-2's licence on CLARIN-IS could not be read. It is likely CC BY 4.0 under the programme default, but this is **unconfirmed**.
- Whether any "Íslenskur grunnorðaforði" list for L2 learners graded A1–B1 is in preparation, for example linked to the national stöðumat, could not be established.
- No subject-specific (discipline) word lists for Icelandic school subjects were found.

## Addendum (2026-10-16): MEMM's framhaldsskóli section and the October 2025 MEMM report, read directly

Method: once network access was widened, every source below was fetched and read directly:

- MEMM's pages via its WordPress API (a browser user agent is needed; plain curl gets 403)
- the progress report PDF and the VMA PDF
- the CLARIN-IS records, Bildetema, PyPI and Tiro

This supersedes the earlier search-based addendum.

### Takeaway

The ministry's own first-year evaluation of the three íslenskubrautir names four gaps: no common curriculum or assessment, no varied and suitable materials for this age group, too little specialised support, and too little funding. MEMM's upper-secondary pages cover reception, placement and teacher guidance, and they list materials. None of the listed material is topic speaking cards with inflection. Picture vocabulary and early literacy are already well covered by MMS. The public Tiro TTS endpoints return 404.

### Cited Findings

**MEMM áfangaskýrsla, Oct 2025** ([PDF](<https://www.stjornarradid.is/library/02-Rit-skyrslur-og-skrar/MRN/afangaskyrsla-MEMM-24-25%20-%20Copy%20(1).pdf>))

- **p. 22, survey of the three schools:**
  - Students progressed in Icelandic and took more part in school and social life; teachers developed their methods.
  - The challenges it names: "Skortur er á samræmdri námskrá og námsmati", "Skortur er á fjölbreyttu og viðeigandi námsefni fyrir þennan aldurshóp", more specialised support needed, and funding lacking.
- **p. 12, the framhaldsskólateymi:**
  - Started August 2024; three teachers at 20 %, an HA professor and an MMS admissions officer.

  - An upper-secondary specialist was hired in May 2025.
- **p. 13:**
  - Stikum af stað was completed in 2025, and training on it has started.
  - Materials mapping is under way.
  - MMS is developing an AI tool, "Björgin", partly to help teachers adapt materials and lesson plans for multilingual students.
- **pp. 16–17:** FÁ offers a block course for newly arrived upper-secondary-age students from autumn 2025.
- **p. 10, general findings:**
  - Placement assessment is needed at all levels.
  - Assessment and tools are needed for inclusion, ISL and multilingualism.
  - Upper secondary needs more varied routes.
- **Appendix 4, forum resolution (MÍ, 2–3 June 2025, 70 participants):** ISL should become a recognised subject in the upper-secondary curriculum, with competence and assessment criteria aligned between grunnskóli and framhaldsskóli against the CEFR. It also calls for materials development to be "efld og henni hraðað".

### Inferences

- **Íslenskubraut answers the evaluation's "materials for this age group" gap directly.** Asking MEMM for a Námsefni listing is justified.
- **Item 4 (talk-to-write) is PWIM applied to the cards**, which aligns it with MEMM's own recommended method.
- **Item 9 (Skólinn)** fits MEMM's three-tier model and the VMA procedure's call for word lists. LÍNO-2 is now cleared to use.
- **Item 5 (audio)** needs a TTS source, because Tiro's public service appears gone or moved.
- **Picture dictionaries and Latin-script or handwriting tools should be linked, not built.** MMS already provides Orðasjóður, Leikur að íslenskum orðum, Skriftarsmiðjan and Staf fyrir staf.

### Gaps

- Whether Tiro moved, or whether another public Icelandic TTS exists.
- Whether every Bildetema theme has Icelandic audio.
- What "Björgin" will do, and when.
