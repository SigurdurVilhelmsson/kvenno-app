## Norway: innføringstilbud, kombinasjonsklasser, grunnleggende norsk, NAFO, Udir mapping, Klar Tale, Morsmål

### Takeaway

Norway pairs an **age-independent, three-level curriculum** (grunnleggende norsk for språklige
minoriteter, usable in both lower and upper secondary) with a **mandatory national mapping tool**
(Udir's kartleggingsverktøy on kgn.udir.no: start-up conversation guides plus five skill areas).
Newly arrived 16-20-year-olds attend introductory offers for up to two years, and some counties run
"combination classes" that teach lower-secondary content alongside upper-secondary subjects. NAFO
(OsloMet) supplies the free multilingual layer: **Bildetema**, a themed picture dictionary with
audio built with Nordic partners **including Iceland**; Tema Morsmål / morsmål.no, with subject
texts in many languages; and LEXIN dictionaries, a Norwegian-Ukrainian one added in 2026. Formal
outcome evaluations of combination classes appear not to exist; Udir's 2025 report found pupils
feel safer speaking Norwegian in separate classes.

### Cited Findings

**Programme organisation**

- Pupils whose mother tongue is not Norwegian or Sami have a right to særskilt språkopplæring until they know enough Norwegian to follow ordinary teaching — [Udir guidance (PDF print of udir.no page, updated 11.10.2023)](https://udir.no/api/PdfApi/PrintPageAsPdfDocument/159378)
- The county has a duty to offer a transition arrangement for newly arrived youth under Education Act § 9-6. Several municipalities and counties run combination classes, where newly arrived youth get lower-secondary teaching alongside upper-secondary subjects. These can meet the duty, but attending one is not a right — [Udir (search excerpt)](https://udir.no/api/PdfApi/PrintPageAsPdfDocument/197711)
- Introductory offers can be given for up to two years under the Education Act — [Udir (search excerpt)](https://udir.no/api/PdfApi/PrintPageAsPdfDocument/213294)
- IMDi published a mapping study in January 2024 (kartlegging av opplæringstilbud). It covers which offers exist, drivers and barriers for setting them up, how young people use them, and the advantages and disadvantages of combination offers, including those for Ukrainian youth — [IMDi report, 12.01.2024](https://arkiv.imdi.no/contentassets/79bd3b668a534f2ba9d2a9b1f7ffc328/20231206_kartlegging-av-opplaringstilbud.-rapport-12-01-2024.pdf)
- Udir's report "Opplæringstilbudet til nyankomne ungdommer fra Ukraina" (31.01.2025, covering autumn 2024) examines how county authorities handle the influx, including recognition of prior schooling and follow-up services — [Udir report](https://udir.no/api/PdfApi/PrintPageAsPdfDocument/215687)
- A newer Udir follow-up study on good teaching for newly arrived pupils found that pupils feel more secure speaking Norwegian in separate classes. Schools find mental-health follow-up harder. Introductory offers fit newly arrived Ukrainians less well than they fit earlier groups — [Udir (search excerpt)](https://udir.no/api/PdfApi/PrintPageAsPdfDocument/215205)
- A CDF research chapter notes a lack of knowledge about how pupils with short residence time experience the transition from introductory offers to ordinary classes — [Cappelen Damm Forskning](https://cdforskning.no/cdf/catalog/view/213/1195/10444)

**Curriculum: grunnleggende norsk for språklige minoriteter**

- The curriculum is age-independent and level-based, with competence aims for **three levels**. It can be used in both grunnskole and videregående opplæring, and the school owner chooses between it and an adapted ordinary Norwegian plan — [NOR7-01 curriculum, data.udir.no](https://data.udir.no/kl06/v201906/laereplaner/NOR7-01.pdf). **Caution:** the version found was marked valid only until 31.07.2020, and a revised plan exists ([Udir consultation](https://hoering.udir.no/LastNedVedlegg/2862)).
- The plan is described as a transition plan, to be used "til eleven har tilstrekkelige ferdigheter i norsk til å følge vanlig opplæring" — [NAFO webinar slides on the curriculum](https://nafo.oslomet.no/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Webinar-om-l%C3%A6replanen-i-grunnleggende-norsk.pdf)
- A level 1 aim in the consultation draft: using "faste formularer for å hilse, introdusere seg, be om noe, takke og uttrykke følelser og behov" — [Udir consultation document](https://hoering.udir.no/LastNedVedlegg/2862)

**Udir's mapping tool (kartleggingsverktøy i grunnleggende norsk)**

- The tool has three purposes: decide whether a pupil needs særskilt språkopplæring, find out which Norwegian teaching they need, and judge when they are ready for ordinary teaching. It consists of **conversation guides for start-up conversations (oppstartsamtaler) and mapping in five skill areas**. It lives at kgn.udir.no; teachers register through UIDP and the school leader assigns their role. Supporting material, including guidance videos, is at OsloMet/NAFO. The person mapping should have competence in Norwegian as a second language — [Udir page as PDF, updated 11.10.2023](https://udir.no/api/PdfApi/PrintPageAsPdfDocument/159378)
- The tool developed for the curriculum shall be used by all school owners who use that curriculum. Other mapping material is allowed with other plans, but it must be able to establish a level equivalent to level 3 — [Språkrådet consultation response](https://www.regjeringen.no/contentassets/3c2a5f7c6a8b4a81a0cf6ff691e6a828/spraakraadet.pdf)

**NAFO resources**

- **Bildetema** is a free multilingual picture dictionary run by NAFO at OsloMet. It has images, text and sound organised by theme, and is meant as a starting point for language learning in kindergartens, schools and adult education. **NAFO's partners are the Swedish Institute for Language and Folklore, the Center of Language and Literacy in Iceland and the National Center for Reading in Denmark** — [About Bildetema, OsloMet (search excerpt)](https://nybildetema.oslomet.no/?p=61); [Bildetema – English, OsloMet](https://film.oslomet.no/bildetema-eng)
- A 2016 article describes Bildetema as an interactive picture dictionary with about **1,900 words in 31 themes and natural speech in 36 languages**. The Norwegian version was based on a Swedish original from the early 2000s, and the word and image material comes from the LEXIN dictionaries. The current site is navigable in six interface languages, so language counts have changed over time. Images are licensed from Adobe Stock — [NSIL article, tidsskrift.dk](https://tidsskrift.dk/nsil/article/download/111216/160266/227518); [About Bildetema](https://nybildetema.oslomet.no/?p=61)
- **LEXIN (Norwegian)** is operated by Uni Computing and funded by Udir. It is based on the state-funded Swedish LEXIN series. A paper describes the illustrated LEXIN dictionaries as rich in content, easy to use, and liked by teachers and students at different levels — [KUDOS document](https://kudos.dfo.no/documents/10272/files/10248.pdf); [LexicoNordica article](https://tidsskrift.dk/lexn/article/download/18427/16082/42022)
- In 2026 OsloMet/NAFO launched a Norwegian-Ukrainian LEXIN. One report gives over 8,000 entries, 1,827 idioms and 9,480 usage examples — [Rubryka, 24.03.2026](https://rubryka.com/en/2026/03/24/ukrayinskyj-onlajn-slovnyk-lexin/) (secondary source)
- **Tema Morsmål** (morsmal.no) is run by NAFO as a multilingual site for kindergartens, schools, children, youth and parents. The grunnskole part has pages in **twelve languages**, and cooperation with the Swedish sister site covers naturfag and samfunnsfag — [search summary of morsmal.no and related pages](https://sprakloyper.uis.no/barnetrinn/flerspraklige-elever-barnetrinn/opplaering-den-forste-tiden-2/nyankomne-minoritetsspraklige-elever/oppgave-til-neste-gang-prov-ut-flerspraklige-nettressurser)
- Teachers can download subject texts in a range of languages and share them with pupils; the site also has multilingual stories — [Statsforvalteren i Agder link list](https://www.statsforvalteren.no/agder/barnehage-og-opplaring/nyheter---barnehage-og-opplaring/nettressurser-og-informasjon-om-korona/)
- A UiS teacher-training task points to Lexin and a Tema Morsmål page of **mathematics concepts in several languages** — [UiS Språkløyper task](https://sprakloyper.uis.no/barnetrinn/flerspraklige-elever-barnetrinn/opplaering-den-forste-tiden-2/nyankomne-minoritetsspraklige-elever/oppgave-til-neste-gang-prov-ut-flerspraklige-nettressurser)
- NAFO also wrote a guide to multilingual work in kindergarten for Udir (2018, revised 2023) — [NAFO veileder PDF](https://nafo.oslomet.no/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/spraklig-mangfold-veileder.pdf)

**Klar Tale**

- Klar Tale is Norway's only easy-to-read newspaper, with large type and simple words. It explicitly addresses readers who are learning Norwegian. The paper edition comes out on Thursdays and there is a podcast, audio, and read-aloud on the web. It has a nynorsk sister edition, Klart & Kort — [Klar Tale, About us](https://www.klartale.no/about-us/); [Wikipedia: Klar Tale](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klar_Tale_(newspaper)>)
- Klart og kort is described as "eit nyttig verktøy i norskundervisninga", and school assignments are built on paper-edition articles — [LNK publication (2013)](https://lnk.no/wp-content/uploads/2008/07/images_doc_AVISA_LNK1_2013.pdf)
- Klar Tale depends on state grants; in 2016 the Braille edition was at risk — [Storting written question](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Sporsmal/Skriftlige-sporsmal-og-svar/Skriftlig-sporsmal/?qnid=40786) (old source)

**Evaluation**

- Udir has an evaluation of Norwegian as a second language ("evaluering av norsk som 2 språk") — [Udir report PDF](https://www.udir.no/globalassets/filer/tall-og-forskning/rapporter/5/evaluering_av_norsk_som_2_sprak.pdf). Its findings could not be read in this session.

### Inferences

- **Bildetema is the single most directly reusable model for Íslenskubraut**, and Iceland's Miðja máls og læsis (the "Center of Language and Literacy in Iceland") is already listed as a NAFO partner. Its design (themes of about 60 words, a photo for each word, recorded native speech, and translation into many home languages) maps closely onto Íslenskubraut's per-topic A1 cards. Check whether an Icelandic Bildetema exists before building a parallel picture vocabulary. Íslenskubraut's ARASAAC chips are the same idea with a different picture licence; ARASAAC is CC BY-NC-SA, while Bildetema's images are commercially licensed stock.
- The **three-level, age-independent curriculum plus a level-3 exit threshold** is a clean model for labelling Íslenskubraut's A1/A2/B1 cards against a "ready for ordinary classes" target.
- Udir's **oppstartsamtale** (structured start-up conversation) is a low-cost, printable mapping idea. Íslenskubraut's guiding questions for oral picture description are already close to a conversation guide.

### Gaps

- The current (post-2020) version of the grunnleggende norsk curriculum, and its exact level descriptors, could not be read.
- I could not confirm whether NAFO / Tema Morsmål has **multilingual subject glossaries for upper-secondary subjects** (naturfag, samfunnsfag) or only for primary school. Only a maths-concepts page is confirmed. The brief's "flerspråklige fagbegrepslister" is unverified.
- I found no independent effectiveness evaluation of combination classes, of Bildetema, or of the Udir mapping tool. The Udir 2025 reports describe practice; they do not measure learning outcomes.
- Morsmål.no's current structure, including whether it has a videregående section, was not readable.
- I could not confirm whether an Icelandic-language version of Bildetema exists today; only the partnership with the Icelandic centre is confirmed.

## Sweden: Språkintroduktion, Svenska som andraspråk, Bygga svenska, Kartläggningsmaterial, Greppa språket, Tema Modersmål, Lexin, 8 Sidor, Skolinspektionen

### Takeaway

Sweden has the most formalised toolkit. Under a 2025 regulation, the **three-step Kartläggningsmaterial** (Step 1: language and experiences; Step 2: literacy and numeracy, done as a conversation and ideally in the mother tongue; Step 3: subject knowledge) is the mandatory basis for admitting a pupil to Språkintroduktion and for writing their individual study plan. **Bygga svenska** is a voluntary five-step assessment support with its own edition for the introduction programmes. Skolinspektionen's 2017 review of 42 schools found that many schools put every pupil on the same timetable regardless of prior knowledge. The lesson the system took from that is to individualise from the mapping, and to link study guidance in the mother tongue (studiehandledning) to subject teaching.

### Cited Findings

**Organisation and mapping**

- Under Skolverket regulation **SKOLFS 2025:453**, Steps 1 and 2 (version 1) of the mapping material must form the basis for assessing a newly arrived pupil's knowledge on admission to språkintroduktion. How much of Steps 1-2 is used is decided case by case — [SKOLFS 2025:453](https://skolfs.skolverket.se/api/document/GRUNDFORFATTNING/2025:453/pdf)
- Step 1 covers language and experiences, with a complementary conversation guide for **pupils of upper-secondary age who have work experience**. Step 2 covers literacy and numeracy as a teacher-pupil conversation, where the numeracy part tests mathematical thinking broadly rather than school maths. On språkintroduktion the Step 2 results feed the individual study plan. Step 3 covers subject knowledge and supports subject teachers, and is done at the school where the pupil is placed — [Skolverket material (search excerpts)](https://pbk-pres-backend.skolverket.se/api/v1/material/download/SKOLV6614331); [Skolverket](https://pbk-pres-backend.skolverket.se/api/v1/material/download/SKOLV6614357)
- In practice, Uppsala's reception unit runs the Step 2 mapping **in the mother tongue**, plus a level test for pupils who were in Swedish school before — [Uppsala routine for placement in språkintroduktion](https://utforareskola.uppsala.se/globalassets/utforare-skola/dokument/overgangar-mellan-skolformer/overgang-arskurs-9-till-gymnasieskola/placera-och-anta-elever-till-sprakintroduktion-ims.pdf)
- Skolverket runs a web course on Steps 1-3 that upper-secondary staff can take — [Skolverket insatslista 2024](https://skolverket.se/download/18.7e9ff56e192dfe241c133/1730382852399/Insatslista_h%C3%B6st_2024_oktober_241031.docx)

**Bygga svenska**

- Bygga svenska is Skolverket's assessment support for all teachers of pupils with Swedish as a second language, from beginner to advanced. It describes language development in **five steps**, with editions for grades 1-3, 4-6, 7-9 and **the introduction programmes in upper secondary**. It is voluntary, can run in parallel with other assessment supports, and was developed by Stockholm University on Skolverket's commission — [Skolverket download](https://pbk-pres-backend.skolverket.se/api/v1/material/download/SKOLV6356721); [Uppsala University, bedömningsstöd](https://www.uu.se/nationella-prov/svenska-och-svenska-som-andrasprak/bedomningsstod)
- It can be used for any second-language pupil, whether newly arrived or in Swedish school for more than four years. It is not designed for needs assessment, though its language analyses can inform one — [Stockholm University NC, bedömningsstöd FAQ](https://www.su.se/enheter/nationellt-centrum-for-svenska-som-andrasprak/rad-och-stod/vanliga-fragor/bedomningsstod-for-svenska-som-andrasprak?cache=568)

**Greppa språket and SKUA**

- _Greppa språket – Ämnesdidaktiska perspektiv på flerspråkighet_ is a free Skolverket research overview of didactic perspectives that benefit multilingual pupils. It is commonly cited as an introduction to SKUA (språk- och kunskapsutvecklande arbetssätt). Lärportalen hosts an upper-secondary module, "Språk- och kunskapsutvecklande arbete", covering every subject (version dated December 2019) — [Skolverket PDF](https://www.skolverket.se/download/18.6bfaca41169863e6a65ad74/1553965452926/pdf3201.pdf); [Lärportalen resource](https://larportalen.skolverket.se/api/resource/P03WCPLAR132294)

**Studiehandledning and Tema Modersmål**

- Skolverket publications: _Studiehandledning på modersmålet – att stödja kunskapsutvecklingen hos flerspråkiga elever_ (2022) and _Digitala resurser i studiehandledning på modersmål_ (2023) — [Lund University reading list](https://www.fil.lu.se/media/utbildning/dokument/kurser/FLRA01/20241/FLRA01.pdf)
- A government directive found that education using pupils' existing languages and knowledge gives better results, and that individualised teaching and collaboration between study guides and subject teachers have been success factors. It also found that mother-tongue teaching often operates on its own, without links to other teaching — [Dir. 2018:38](https://lagen.nu/dir/2018:38)

**Lexin and 8 Sidor**

- Lexin began in the late 1970s as a Skolöverstyrelsen project at Göteborg University, building bilingual dictionaries for newly arrived immigrants — [Språkbanken resource description](https://svn.spraakbanken.gu.se/sb-arkiv/pub/resurstext/lexin_eng.html). Isof (Institutet för språk och folkminnen) hosts it; the Swedish-English version covers everyday words and terms tied to Swedish society — [SND dataset record](https://researchdata.se/en/catalogue/dataset/ext0286-1)
- 8 Sidor is an easy-to-read Swedish news site (8sidor.se) and print paper. Myndigheten för tillgängliga medier (MTM) took over publishing on 1.1.2015, and it has an independent responsible publisher. MTM lists people learning Swedish among its target groups — [Dir. 2018:4](https://lagen.nu/dir/2018:4); [MTM brochure 2024](https://www.mtm.se/app/uploads/2024/09/Myndigheten-for-tillgangliga-medier-27100-webb.pdf)

**Evaluation: Skolinspektionen**

- Skolinspektionen's 2017 quality review of språkintroduktion covered **42 schools**. Many did not meet pupils' needs: pupils often studied few subjects on the same timetable regardless of prior knowledge. The Director-General said collective solutions had been prioritised over individual needs. Almost 36,000 pupils were in the programme in autumn 2016 — [Stockholm University NC summary](https://www.su.se/enheter/nationellt-centrum-for-svenska-som-andrasprak/forskning-och-konferenser/rapporter/skolinspektionens-granskning-sprakintro-maste-forbattras); [Skolinspektionen report](https://www.Skolinspektionen.SE/globalassets/02-beslut-rapporter-stat/granskningsrapporter/tkg/2017/sprakintroduktion/sprakintroduktion-i-gymnasieskolan.pdf)
- I found no newer Skolinspektionen review dedicated to språkintroduktion. A 2024 consultation response mentions several integration-related reviews without specifying this programme — [Skolinspektionen remissvar](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/a2353b21f0af4442a6ad8ace67df5037/skolinspektionen.pdf)

### Inferences

- The Swedish **Step 1 conversation guide for older pupils with work experience** is directly relevant to 16-20-year-olds. An Íslenskubraut "kortlagning" card, a structured printable conversation on languages, schooling and work experience, would borrow it cheaply.
- Skolinspektionen's main criticism was one-size-fits-all scheduling. For a card tool, that argues for letting a teacher **mix levels and topics per pupil** rather than fixing a sequence.
- SKUA and studiehandledning both stress **linking language to subject content**. Íslenskubraut's current topics (animals, food, vehicles, people, places, clothing) are everyday A1 vocabulary. Subject-bridging topics, such as school-subject or vocational vocabulary, would be the next step if the goal is entering upper secondary.

### Gaps

- **Lexin "bildteman"** (thematic picture pages in the Swedish Lexin) could not be confirmed by any search result. The illustrated LEXIN dictionaries are confirmed, but the thematic picture section is not. Bildetema itself is a Swedish original (Isof partnership), which may be what "bildteman" refers to.
- I could not read the current Tema Modersmål site (modersmal.skolverket.se): its languages and whether it serves upper-secondary pupils.
- The Bygga svenska five-step descriptors themselves, and any Skolinspektionen or academic evaluation of Bygga svenska or of the mapping material's effect, were not found.
- No evaluation of 8 Sidor's use in språkintroduktion was found.

## Denmark: dansk som andetsprog at youth level (FGU, EUD, modtagelsesklasser), EMU and the Ministry

### Takeaway

Denmark's youth-level offer is fragmented, and the least documented in this search. Late arrivals can be in reception classes or "ungehold" (youth groups) within folkeskole. At 16-20 the main routes are **FGU**, the preparatory basic education where Danish as a second language (DSA) is an option and new introductory-level DSA lines are being set up, and **EUD**, where "erhvervsrettet andetsprogsdansk" is a basic subject with ministry guidance and an emu.dk community, "emu-respons". EVA has evaluated basisundervisning in primary school and FGU overall, but I found no evaluation of DSA at FGU or EUD.

### Cited Findings

- Hedensted municipality describes an "ungehold" track for newly arrived 14-17-year-olds who cannot follow ordinary classes in Danish. Intake is continuous, the ideal group is about 15, and pupils join ordinary classes fully or partly when it makes sense — [Hedensted decision document (2016)](https://www.hedensted.dk/hedensted_data/dagsorden/Udvalget_for_Laering_14-17/06-06-2016/ID1737/Bilag/Punkt_44_Bilag_1_Ungehold_Maal_og_rammer_for_et_saerligt_undervisningstilbud_som_alternativ_til_modtagelsesklasser.pdf)
- Nyborg runs a reception class for pupils who arrived after age 14, teaching basic DSA, without the two-year limit that applies elsewhere — [Nyborg agenda](https://dagsordener.nyborg.dk/pdf/GetDagsorden/42092b60-f9dd-4c9e-8be7-504810a48e65)
- A Folketing document proposed making DSA an option on all FGU lines. The basic track introduces the education track and teaches Danish or DSA and mathematics — [ft.dk](https://www.ft.dk/samling/20161/almdel/UNU/bilag/141/1782337.pdf)
- A recent FGU job ad seeks a DSA teacher for a **new introductory-level line**, for young people who have often been in reception classes but are not ready for G-level teaching — [Jobindex ad](https://www.jobindex.dk/jobannonce/h1677822/dsa-underviser-til-ny-linje-paa-introducerende-niveau-paa-fgu) (a job ad, not an official source)
- The Ministry (STUK) guide to **erhvervsrettet andetsprogsdansk** explains the subject's place in EUD and its relation to other subjects; it is non-binding guidance — [UVM guidance 2019](https://www.uvm.dk/-/media/filer/uvm/udd/erhverv/pdf19/jul/190702-erhvervsrettet-andetsprogsdansk.pdf)
- The 2023 Danish guidance points to **emu-respons**, a professional community for EUD basic subjects where teachers can ask the subject consultant questions and share materials — [UVM vejledning dansk 2023](https://www.uvm.dk/-/media/filer/uvm/udd/erhverv/pdf23/maj/230516-vejledning-dansk.pdf)
- The adult and AMU "Fagunderstøttende dansk" courses can be run as Danish teaching alone or together with subject teaching — [NNF pamphlet 2024](https://www.nnf.dk/media/te1dc3s0/pjece-dansk_web_2024.pdf) (adult or AMU context, not EUD)
- **Evaluations:** EVA mapped how 20 municipalities organise basisundervisning for bilingual pupils, then studied six schools. Teachers found it hard to cover other subjects while pupils learned a new language — [UVM/EVA 2016](https://www.uvm.dk/-/media/filer/uvm/publikationer/folkeskolen/2016-basisundervisning-for-tosprogede-elever.pdf); [EVA six-schools report 2019](https://www.uvm.dk/-/media/filer/uvm/publikationer/2019/dec/191204-basisundervisning-i-dansk-som-andetsprog-pa-seks-skoler.pdf)
- EVA also studied direct enrolment of newly arrived pupils (aged 5-18) in ordinary classes — [EVA 2019](https://www.eva.dk/sites/eva/files/2019-11/MASTER_Unders%C3%B8gelse%20af%20direkte%20indskrivning%20af%20nyankomne%20elever%20i%20almenklasser_081119.pdf) — and evaluated Hillerød's DSA basisundervisning, which uses DSA advisers in the home classes — [UVM 2018](https://uvm.dk/media/sbinngal/181211-evaluering-af-basisundervisningen-i-dansk-som-andetsprog-hilleroed-kommune.pdf)
- A 2023 EVA evaluation found FGU nationally "shows good signs" at getting more young people into work, with room to improve retention of pupils who go on to youth education — [ft.dk bilag](https://www.ft.dk/samling/20231/almdel/BUU/bilag/97/2819776.pdf)

### Inferences

- The Danish material points to a **vocationally anchored** model (erhvervsrettet andetsprogsdansk) more than a resource bank. For Íslenskubraut, the transferable idea is that A2/B1 cards could carry trade or workplace vocabulary for students heading to vocational tracks.
- **Direct enrolment with DSA advisers in the home class** (Hillerød) contrasts with Norway's separate classes. The Nordic systems disagree on separation versus immersion, so a tool should work in both settings.

### Gaps

- No emu.dk page itself was returned. I could not confirm specific free EMU learner resources for DSA at youth level, such as glossaries, audio or easy-language texts.
- No evaluation of DSA at FGU or EUD was found, and the EVA reports' detailed findings could not be read.
- I found no Danish equivalent of Bildetema, Lexin or 8 Sidor in this search. The Danish National Center for Reading (Nationalt Videncenter for Læsning) is a Bildetema partner, which suggests Bildetema has a Danish version, but that is unconfirmed.

## Finland: S2, valmistava opetus, TUVA, Opetushallitus, Kielibuusti, Selkokieli/Selkokeskus, Jyväskylä research

### Takeaway

Finland moved 16-20-year-old newcomers into **TUVA** (tutkintokoulutukseen valmentava koulutus): a one-year (38-week) programme preparing students for upper secondary or vocational study, with national core requirements published in 2021 and teaching from autumn 2022. Strengthening the language of study is an explicit goal, and providers run S2-supported TUVA lines. OPH's main free teacher resource is about **language awareness and multiliteracy across subjects**, not a learner bank. **Selkokieli** (plain Finnish), backed by Selkokeskus, is the easy-language layer. The strongest evaluation evidence is Jyväskylä-Tampere research on lower-secondary valmistava opetus: reading comprehension mostly reached the target and writing lagged, and the researchers judged the target level too low for further study. "Kielibuusti" is a university-sector project for international professionals, not a youth resource.

### Cited Findings

- OPH published the TUVA core requirements in 2021. TUVA serves young people finishing basic education and adults who need preparatory education, and local TUVA teaching began in autumn 2022 — [OPH news 2021](https://www.oph.fi/fi/uutiset/2021/tutkintokoulutukseen-valmentavan-koulutuksen-perusteet-julkaistu)
- One TUVA goal is to strengthen the student's skills in the language of study and qualification, so they are ready for upper-secondary study. Language awareness in TUVA means recognising language's role in teaching and learning, and it is tied to multiliteracy, one of TUVA's broad competence areas — [OPH: Kielitietoisuus TUVA-koulutuksessa](https://www.oph.fi/fi/koulutus-ja-tutkinnot/kielitietoisuus-tuva-koulutuksessa)
- OPH's free teacher material _Monilukutaitoa kielitietoisesti eri oppiaineissa – tietoa ja työtapoja_ has subject-specific sections and a video. It was updated in 2025 with tips on AI tools, and an English version exists — [OPH material](https://www.oph.fi/fi/oppimateriaali/monilukutaitoa-kielitietoisesti-eri-oppiaineissa-tietoa-ja-tyotapoja)
- Example provider: Riihimäki (Hyria) runs an **S2-supported TUVA** that requires at least A1.3. It is 38 weeks, with an optional 0-30-week part preparing for lukio. The A1.3 entry requirement is that provider's own condition, not a national rule — [Hyria](https://www.hyria.fi/print/pdf/node/7299)
- An Espoo draft statement notes that many TUVA students' language skills are still developing, and argues that a language-level entry requirement would be problematic without an alternative for students who do not meet it — [Espoo minutes 2021](https://espoo-sv.oncloudos.com/kokous/2021276-6-33732.PDF)
- In Kannus, one TUVA path serves students whose Finnish is not yet sufficient for upper-secondary degree study — [Kannus minutes 2024](https://kannus10.oncloudos.com/kokous/2024324-3-11657.PDF)
- OKM tasked OPH with updating the basic-education S2 syllabus by 30.1.2026, with local curricula in use by 1.8.2026. Group-based support teaching in the language of instruction was introduced in basic education from the start of 2025 — [Valtioneuvosto](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1410845/maahanmuuttotaustaisten-oppilaiden-suomen-ja-ruotsin-osaamista-vahvistetaan) (basic education, not TUVA)
- OPH merged with the Kotimaisten kielten keskus (Kotus) on 1.1.2026 — [OPH news 2026](https://www.oph.fi/fi/uutiset/2026/opetushallituksen-tilinpaatos-2025-suuria-saastoja-ja-virastojen-yhdistyminen)
- A 2025 University of Lapland master's thesis found that the main obstacles to language-aware teaching in TUVA were lack of resources and teaching arrangements, along with language and cultural differences — [Peltola 2025](https://lauda.ulapland.fi/bitstream/10024/66886/1/Peltola_Virpi.pdf)

**Kielibuusti (the brief's "Kielibuusteri")**

- Kielibuusti.fi is a joint Aalto University and University of Helsinki project funded by the Opetus- ja kulttuurirahasto for 2025-2028. It gathers materials for learning Finnish and Swedish, for learners, teachers, guidance counsellors and international workplaces, and it maintains a national network of higher-education teaching of Finnish and Swedish as second languages. It received the Kari Sajavaara recognition award — [Aalto news](https://www.aalto.fi/fi/uutiset/kari-sajavaara-tunnustuspalkinto-kielibuusti-hankkeelle-yhteiskunnallisesti-vaikuttavasta-kieliin); [Aalto Language Centre projects](https://www.aalto.fi/fi/kielikeskus/hankkeet-ja-ajankohtaista)
- A 2026 Innokylä guide lists InfoFinland, Kielibuusti, Yle Oppiminen and Finsku as free Finnish-learning sites — [Innokylä PDF](https://www.innokyla.fi/sites/default/files/2026-06/Vuorovaikutus%20%26%20selkokieli.pdf)

**Selkokieli / Selkokeskus**

- Selkokeskus publishes plain-language material, trains users, and runs a text adaptation and evaluation service. It may award the **selkotunnus** (plain-language logo) to texts that meet the criteria — [journal.fi article](https://journal.fi/mediaviestinta/article/download/100622/58137/178221)
- Immigrants studying Finnish are among the groups needing plain language, estimated at 11-14% of Finland's population — [University of Helsinki](https://www.helsinki.fi/fi/uutiset/kulttuuri/selkokieli-saavutettavuutta)
- A 2025 Turku master's thesis surveyed S2 teachers. Textbooks, fiction and news are the most-used plain-language formats, and the basic level of selkokieli is used most — [Säynäjäkangas, UTU](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstream/10024/181700/1/Saynajakangas_Emilia_opinnayte.pdf)
- A Jyväskylä thesis analysed plain language in the S2 textbook _Suomen mestari 1_, noting that selkokieli supports, and does not replace, standard Finnish — [JYX record](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/59549)

**Jyväskylä research**

- A Jyväskylä-Tampere longitudinal study of **lower-secondary valmistava opetus**, funded by OKM, found that almost 9 in 10 participating pupils exceeded the target level in reading comprehension, while writing developed more slowly. The researchers consider the current target level **too low for further study** — [JYU news](https://www.jyu.fi/fi/uutinen/tutkijat-seurasivat-vuoden-ylakoulun-valmistavaa-opetusta-vasta-maahan-muuttaneiden-kielitaito)
- The **POLKU** project follows recently arrived youth as they move from valmistava opetus into basic education or upper secondary, using quantitative data from 2024 and 2025 plus interviews and ethnography — [JYU project page](https://www.jyu.fi/fi/hankkeet/vasta-maahan-saapuneiden-oppiminen-kiinnittyminen-ja-osallisuus)
- Mirja Tarnanen (JYU, 2025): research on smoothing the transition from valmistava opetus to basic education, including a new pedagogical assessment tool under development — [JYU article](https://www.jyu.fi/fi/artikkeli/integroituminen-on-kahden-kauppa-valmiuksia-vaaditaan-oppilaalta-ja-koululta)
- Teräs, Lasonen & Cools (2015) found that cooperation between authorities, homes and young people is central to preventing exclusion in immigrant youth's educational transitions — [journal.fi](https://journal.fi/akakk/article/download/90086/49277/144842)
- An older Virittäjä review of S2 web materials criticised them as mechanistic and often made for a narrow target group. It praised Supisuomea as rich for differentiated teaching — [Virittäjä](https://journal.fi/virittaja/article/download/40395/9821/27903) (dated)

### Inferences

- The Jyväskylä finding that **writing lags reading and that targets set too low strand students** argues for Íslenskubraut's sentence builder: productive, structured writing support at A1/A2 is the weak spot the research names.
- Selkokieli's **certified-level** approach (a selkotunnus plus graded difficulty levels) is a model for labelling Íslenskubraut texts by difficulty in a way a teacher can trust.
- Finland's national resources for this age group are teacher-development material (language awareness), not learner banks. A small tool can fill the learner-facing gap.

### Gaps

- The TUVA core requirements' exact S2 content, and any national language-level target, were not read. No national B1 entry or exit requirement was found.
- I could not read Selkokeskus's own pages (selkokeskus.fi, Selkosanomat) for current services.
- No evaluation of TUVA's language outcomes was found, and the POLKU results are not yet published (or not found).
- No evidence that Kielibuusti offers anything aimed at 16-20-year-olds.

## Estonia: Estonian as a second language (Keeleklikk, Keeletee, Harno, transition to Estonian-medium schooling)

### Takeaway

Estonia's flagship free resources are **Keeleklikk** (0-A2, 16 chapters, about 1,200 exercises, animations, grammar videos, and a real teacher learners can email) and **Keeletee** (A2-B1, 13 chapters with authentic interviews and tests). They were funded by ESF and the Ministry, have English, Russian and (per one page) Ukrainian interfaces, and are aimed at adults, though the Ministry says teachers can use them with children. Harno runs Estonian proficiency exams and assessment. Youth policy is dominated by the **transition to Estonian-medium schooling**: lower secondary must switch by 2029/30 and gymnasium from 2030/31, with B2 and C1 targets. Research reports a materials shortage, and that vocabulary lists, sentence starters and visual "talking walls" help pupils most. Refugee youth (mainly Ukrainian) face barriers to vocational and higher education. No independent effectiveness evaluation of Keeleklikk was found.

### Cited Findings

- **Keeleklikk:** A2 target; 16 chapters with animations, grammar videos and about 1,200 exercises. **Keeletee:** B1 target; 13 chapters with authentic interviews, grammar videos, exercises and tests — [Keeleklikk info page (search excerpt)](https://keeleklikk.ee/info_en.html); [Keeletee info page](https://www.keeletee.ee/info_en.html)
- Both courses include an Estonian teacher learners can contact by email, and Keeleklikk supports exchanging letters and oral messages with that teacher — [Global Estonian FAQ](https://globalestonian.com/en/node/8126); [DreamApply news](https://estonia.dreamapply.com/news/new/12-online-estonian-course-english-and-russian-speakers)
- Interface languages: the Keeletee page lists English and Russian; the Keeleklikk page also lists Ukrainian — [Keeletee](https://www.keeletee.ee/info_en.html); [Keeleklikk](https://keeleklikk.ee/info_en.html) (the two sources differ)
- The Ministry says Keeleklikk can also be used by teachers to teach children — [HM language study materials](https://hm.ee/en/estonian-and-foreign-languages/estonian-language/language-study-materials)
- Funded by the European Social Fund and the Ministry of Education and Research, free on computers and mobile devices. It won a ministry award and the European Commission's award for contributions to foreign-language teaching (European Language Label) — [Estonian World](https://estonianworld.com/?p=4272); [eesti.ca](https://www.eesti.ca/estonian-teaching-goes-online-with-free-course/article35612)
- Usage: a 2013 Ministry announcement said the Russian-language version had over 12,000 active users and 4,300 homework submissions — [DreamApply news](https://estonia.dreamapply.com/news/new/12-online-estonian-course-english-and-russian-speakers) (self-reported, old)
- Harno conducts school exams and assessment tests, Estonian proficiency exams and citizenship exams, and develops assessment tools — [Harno yearbook 2024-2025 (EPALE)](https://epale.ec.europa.eu/et/resource-centre/content/harno-aastaraamat-2024-2025)

- ERR reported 9,260 Ukrainian children and young people in Estonian education in one month. - Vocational pathways include a flexible introductory course, usually 6-12 months, with an individual curriculum for each learner — [HM guidance for Ukrainian children and youth](https://hm.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2022-08/ua_lapse_ja_noore_hariduse_jatkamine_08_03_lapsevanem_22_03.pdf)

### Inferences

- Keeleklikk's **human-tutor-in-the-loop** feature (emailing a real teacher) and its **story-driven, animated chapters** are its distinctive design choices. Its evidence is awards and usage counts, not measured learning.
- The Estonian classroom finding, that **vocabulary lists + sentence starters + visual supports** help most, is an independent endorsement of Íslenskubraut's card design (vocabulary, sentence frames, pictures).

### Gaps

- No independent effectiveness evaluation of Keeleklikk or Keeletee was found.
- I could not read Harno's own pages or E-koolikott. Whether Harno provides free youth-specific Estonian-as-a-second-language material (beyond exams) is unconfirmed.
- No information was found on immigrant (non-Russian, non-Ukrainian) 16-20-year-olds specifically, or on vocational-school language support.

## Cross-cutting: recurring design features, and which have been evaluated

### Takeaway

Seven features recur across the five systems:

1. a **structured intake mapping**, often a conversation and often in the mother tongue (Norway's oppstartsamtale and five skill areas; Sweden's three steps);
2. a **level- or step-based progression independent of age**, with an explicit exit threshold (Norway's three levels with level 3 as the gate; Bygga svenska's five steps; Keeleklikk A2 → Keeletee B1);
3. **themed picture vocabulary with native-speaker audio and home-language translations** (Bildetema, built jointly by Norway, Sweden, Iceland and Denmark; illustrated LEXIN);
4. **easy-language news and texts** (Klar Tale, 8 Sidor, Selkokieli with Selkosanomat);
5. **use of the first language for subject learning** (Tema Morsmål / Modersmål, studiehandledning);
6. **language-aware teaching across all subjects** (SKUA / Greppa språket; OPH's kielitietoisuus);
7. **scaffolds for production**: vocabulary lists, sentence starters, visual walls.

Evaluation evidence is thin and mostly about **organisation**, not resources. Skolinspektionen found individualisation lacking. The Swedish directive found first-language use and study-guide/teacher collaboration to be success factors. The Finnish longitudinal study found writing lags reading and targets are too low. Udir found pupils feel safer speaking in separate classes. EVA found it hard to cover subject content during basisundervisning. **No learner resource itself (Bildetema, Lexin, Keeleklikk, 8 Sidor, Klar Tale) had an independent effectiveness study in the results found.**

### Cited Findings

- Sweden's directive: using pupils' existing languages gives better results; individualised teaching and study-guide/subject-teacher collaboration are success factors; mother-tongue teaching is often isolated — [Dir. 2018:38](https://lagen.nu/dir/2018:38)
- Skolinspektionen: collective solutions were prioritised over individual needs — [SU NC summary](https://www.su.se/enheter/nationellt-centrum-for-svenska-som-andrasprak/forskning-och-konferenser/rapporter/skolinspektionens-granskning-sprakintro-maste-forbattras)
- Finland: reading exceeded targets for almost 90% of pupils, writing lagged, and targets are too low for further study — [JYU](https://www.jyu.fi/fi/uutinen/tutkijat-seurasivat-vuoden-ylakoulun-valmistavaa-opetusta-vasta-maahan-muuttaneiden-kielitaito)
- Norway: pupils feel more secure speaking Norwegian in separate classes — [Udir](https://udir.no/api/PdfApi/PrintPageAsPdfDocument/215205)
- Estonia: vocabulary lists, sentence starters and talking walls were the most helpful supports — [UT thesis](https://dspace.ut.ee/bitstreams/0df81f4e-f45a-4fa4-b713-c62798fae459/download)
- Bildetema is a joint Nordic product with an Icelandic partner, and its content derives from LEXIN — [About Bildetema](https://nybildetema.oslomet.no/?p=61); [NSIL article](https://tidsskrift.dk/nsil/article/download/111216/160266/227518)
- The illustrated LEXIN dictionaries are reported to be liked by teachers and students at different levels (user-perspective study, not an outcome study) — [LexicoNordica](https://tidsskrift.dk/lexn/article/download/18427/16082/42022); [LEXIN user-perspective article](https://tidsskrift.dk/lexn/article/view/18973)

### Inferences

Candidate borrowings for Íslenskubraut, ranked by fit with what it already has:

1. **Native-speaker audio per word and sentence**, as in Bildetema and Keeleklikk. Íslenskubraut has no audio today, and audio is the most common feature it lacks.
2. **Home-language glosses on A1 vocabulary**, as in Bildetema, LEXIN and Tema Morsmål. This is a bilingual glossary per card, perhaps for the largest groups at Kvennó (for example Ukrainian, Arabic, Polish, Spanish and English). It is supported by the Swedish first-language finding.
3. **A printable intake conversation card** (Norway's oppstartsamtale; Sweden's Step 1 for older pupils with work experience) to map languages, prior schooling and interests before choosing a level.
4. **Explicit level descriptors and an exit threshold** (Norway's level 3; Bygga svenska's five steps), so a teacher can say what B1 on the cards means for entering ordinary upper-secondary classes.
5. **Short graded easy-Icelandic texts** on each card topic (Klar Tale, 8 Sidor, selkokieli). Icelandic has no state-backed easy-news equivalent that I found evidence of here; that is a gap, not a finding.
6. **More productive (writing/speaking) scaffolding**, which is where the sentence builder fits, given the Finnish evidence that writing lags.
7. **Subject- and vocation-bridging topics** (SKUA, Denmark's erhvervsrettet andetsprogsdansk), so the B1 cards move toward school-subject language.

The **lowest-effort, highest-fit action** is to check whether Bildetema already has Icelandic, since Iceland's Miðja máls og læsis is a listed partner. If it does, Íslenskubraut could link to it per topic rather than rebuilding picture vocabulary and audio.

### Gaps

- No controlled or independent outcome evaluation of any of the named digital or printable learner resources was found in this search.
- No cross-national comparative review (for example Nordic Council, OECD or Eurydice) of these youth programmes was retrieved; one would strengthen the cross-cutting claims.
- Every finding rests on search excerpts, because direct fetching was blocked in this environment (see the method note at the top).
