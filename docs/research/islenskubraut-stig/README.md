# Íslenskubraut's levels against the official curricula

Reviewed on 2026-11-12 against the two curricula Siggi supplied:

- _Íslenska fyrir útlendinga – grunnnám_ (Menntamálaráðuneyti, 2008): four 60-hour courses, ÍFÚ 1–4,
  that together "þjálfa þá færni sem samsvarar viðmiðunarrömmum A1 – A2" (§1.3).
- _Íslenska fyrir útlendinga – framhaldsnámsskrá_ (2011): five 60-hour courses. ÍFÚ 5–7 aim at B1,
  and ÍFÚ 8–9 at B1–B2 (Skipan náms).

The ministry does not publish either file at a stable address that this repo links to, so the
quotations below give section headings rather than page numbers.

## What the curricula can and cannot settle

**Neither curriculum lists words by level.** Both level by topic (_efnisatriði_) and by what the
learner can do (_loka- og færnimarkmið_), and the 2008 one says outright that it does not tie
grammar to a stage: "Hér verður ekki bent á að nemendur þurfi, á ákveðnu stigi námsins, að hafa
ákveðna málfræðiþætti á valdi sínu" (§1.1). No official Icelandic word list graded by CEFR level
turned up in a search either. LÍNO-2 and MÍNO, which Skólinn already uses, rank school words by
frequency, not by level. So a word's level here is a judgement: the topic the curriculum puts it
under, how basic the word is within that topic, and what the card's frames can use it for.

**The 2008 curriculum does not say where A1 ends.** It gives four courses for A1–A2 together. This
review takes ÍFÚ 1–2 as A1 and ÍFÚ 3–4 as A2. That is an inference: §1.2 says the first two
courses teach "grunnorðaforða" and the last two go "dýpra í sömu þætti", which fits that split.

**Both curricula are written for adults.** They lean on work, banks, the post office, renting a
flat and tax returns. Íslenskubraut is for teenagers in school. Where the two disagree, this review
follows the students' needs and says so (Skólinn, below).

## The frames: in line

The sentence frames, which the cards treat as the main difference between the levels, match the
curricula well.

- **A1** frames are single clauses (`Þetta er ___.`, `Það er ___.`, `Það hefur ___.`). That is
  ÍFÚ 1–2: simple statements about familiar things.
- **A2** frames join two facts (`Þetta er ___ sem býr ___.`, `Það hefur ___ og ___.`,
  `Hún/Hann vinnur sem ___.`). That is ÍFÚ 3–4: describing at more length.
- **B1** frames give reasons and comparisons (`Ég held að þetta sé ___ vegna þess að ___.`,
  `Það er líkt ___ en ólíkt ___.`, Skólinn's `Ég er sammála/ósammála af því að ___.`). That is
  ÍFÚ 5–7 almost word for word: _útskýrir skoðanir sínar_, _rökstyður_.

So the levels differ mainly in what the learner does with the words, and that is what the
curricula ask for. It also means B1 should not add many new nouns to the Orðaforði page. After
this review only four words start there.

## The Orðaforði levels: revised

The first draft (same day) gave each word the first level whose answers, frames or example used it.
That copied the Spurningaspjald's own levels, including where those are at odds with the
curricula, and it split groups in ways no teacher would: three colours at A2 and the rest at A1,
`syndir` at A2 but `flýgur` at B1.

The revision puts each **group** where the curriculum puts its topic, and then moves later only the
words that are less basic within the topic. No word now starts later than the level where the card
first uses it, checked by comparing every word with the card's answers, frames and examples by BÍN lemma. After the CEFR check below
moved six words earlier, the count is 166 words at A1, 63 from A2 and 4 from B1, out of 233.

## The Spurningaspjald's answers: four recommendations, not applied

_Items 3 and 4 were revised after the CEFR check; see the section after this one._

The question card's answer lists are levelled in the YAML and printed. Changing them also touches
the A1 pictograms (`myndir/myndir.yaml` needs an entry for every A1 chip), the Polish glosses, the
recordings and the guessing games, so these are recommendations for Siggi and not part of the PR.

1. **Colours.** The Útlit questions on Matur and Klæðnaður offer four or five colours at A1 and add
   `gult`, `bleikt`, `brúnt` (and `appelsínugult` on Matur) at A2. The curriculum puts colours together at
   A1 (ÍFÚ 2). Recommend moving them all to A1.
2. **Clothing.** Klæðnaður's A1 answers are `bolur`, `buxur`, `jakki`, `skór`, with `peysa`,
   `skyrta`, `pils`, `úlpa`, `sokkar`, `húfa` at A2. Clothing is ÍFÚ 2, and in Iceland `úlpa` and
   `húfa` are needed in the first week. Recommend all ten at A1, with `hanskar` and `trefill` at A2.
3. **Seasons and days.** The "hvenær" box on every card but Skólinn puts `á veturna`, `á sumrin`
   and `um helgar` at A2, and five of them `á virkum dögum` too. Time words, days and months are ÍFÚ 1. Recommend A1.
4. **Shape and touch at A1.** Lögun (`kringlótt`, `ferkantað`, `ílangt`, `flatt`) and Áferð
   (`slétt`, `gróft`) are at A1, Lögun on five cards and Áferð on three. Neither topic appears in ÍFÚ 1–2, and both are less
   frequent than the colours waiting at A2. Recommend moving Lögun to A2 and keeping only
   `mjúkt`/`hart` from Áferð at A1. This frees A1 space for items 1–3.

## The CEFR: no Icelandic word list, but a usable English proxy

Checked on 2026-11-12, at Siggi's request.

**The CEFR itself grades no words.** Its vocabulary scales describe range, not content: at A1 "a
basic vocabulary repertoire of isolated words and phrases related to particular concrete
situations", at A2 enough "to conduct routine, everyday transactions involving familiar situations
and topics", at B1 enough to manage "with some circumlocutions on most topics pertinent to everyday
life" (Companion Volume, _Vocabulary range_). Word lists come from each language's own _Reference
Level Descriptions_, built from frequency, corpora, learner exams and expert judgement. There are
RLDs for English, German (_Profile Deutsch_), French, Spanish, Italian and others, and **none for
Icelandic**. So the CEFR cannot settle an Icelandic word's level directly, which agrees with what the
ÍFÚ curricula already showed.

**What it can do is give a second opinion by concept.** Cambridge English grades its exam word lists
by CEFR level: _Pre A1 Starters_, _A1 Movers_ and _A2 Flyers_ (for children aged 6 to 12), _A2 Key_ and
_B1 Preliminary_. The young learners' lists suit Íslenskubraut better than the adult ÍFÚ topics do,
since they are built around school, home, animals and play. School things (`book`, `pen`, `ruler`,
`desk`, `classroom`) are _Pre A1 Starters_ words there, which backs keeping Skólinn's basic words at A1
against ÍFÚ 4. Every Orðaforði concept was looked up in
them. English is not Icelandic, and a sledge is rarer in Britain than here, so this is a check on
the curriculum placement and not a replacement for it.

What it showed:

- **Mostly agreement.** The words held back to A2 or B1 are late in the English lists too: `mammal`,
  `reptile`, `amphibian`, `beak` and `horn` appear in none up to B1; `insect`, `wing`, `sledge`,
  `engine`, `fast`, `singer`, `artist`, `museum` and `glove` are A2 (Flyers or Key); `politician`,
  `bald`, `alive`, `elderly`, `raw` and `frozen` first appear at B1; `church`, `suburb` and `fictional`
  appear nowhere. The cooking words (`fried`, `boiled`, `bake`) are A2 (Key).
- **Six words moved earlier, to A1.** `fly`, `swim`, `run`, `motorbike`, `train` and `playground` are
  _Pre A1 Starters_ words, the very first level. The first draft had put Dýr's movement verbs at A2
  only because the card's A2 example uses `syndir`, while Staðir already had `syndir` at A1, so the
  two cards disagreed. `mótorhjól`, `lest` and `leikvöllur` had been held back by the curriculum's
  topic (transport and play are not named in ÍFÚ 1–2), which is weak evidence against a word a
  six-year-old English learner meets first. Moving a word earlier cannot break the rule that no
  word starts after the card first uses it.
- **Left alone, with the disagreement noted for Siggi.** `scarf`, `beard`, `hospital`, `mountain` and
  `famous` are _A1 Movers_ words, so `trefill`, `með skegg`, `sjúkrahús`, `í fjöllunum` and `fræg` could
  start at A1 too. The curriculum puts them later (ÍFÚ 3–4), and A1 Movers sits at the top of A1, so
  they stay at A2 unless Siggi prefers otherwise. In the other direction, `sour` and `bitter` first
  appear at B1 and `soft`, `hard`, the seasons and `restaurant` at A2, while the cards print `súrt`,
  `beiskt`, `mjúkt`, `hart`, `á veturna`, `á sumrin` and `veitingastaður` at A1. Those are taught with the
  A1 frames on the card, so they stay.

**What it changes in the four recommendations.** Colours (1) are all _Pre A1 Starters_ words, so that
recommendation is stronger. Clothing (2) holds: `jacket`, `trousers`, `shoe`, `sock`, `shirt`, `skirt`,
`T-shirt` and `hat` are Starters, `coat`, `sweater` and `scarf` Movers, and only `glove` waits for A2.
Seasons (3) is weaker: `weekend` is A1 but the four seasons are A2 in the English lists, so the case
for `á veturna` and `á sumrin` at A1 rests on Icelandic winters alone, which is a fair case but
Siggi's to make. Shape and touch (4) needs correcting: `round` and `square` are A1 (Movers) and
`soft` and `hard` A2, the opposite of what it proposed, while `smooth` and `rough` are B1, which
supports taking `slétt` and `gróft` out of A1. The better version is to keep `kringlótt` and
`ferkantað` at A1 and move `slétt` and `gróft` to A2. `ílangt` and `flatt` have no clear English
counterpart in the lists (`long` is Starters, but means something else), so they stay a judgement call.

## Academic words

Several groups are school-subject vocabulary that no adult curriculum reaches: Dýr's biological
classes and diets, the B1 shapes (`sporöskjulaga`, `sívalningslaga`) and materials
(`úr endurunnu efni`), and Skólinn's B1 "Námsorð". The research report in
`docs/research/islenskubraut-additions/` argues these belong in the course anyway, because the
students have to move into school Icelandic. Placing them at A2–B1, as the cards do, is consistent
with that.
